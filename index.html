FadeShed is where I publish software I first built to solve my own problems — and then decided might solve yours too.

So much of the software I rely on exists because someone, somewhere, chose to share their code, their notes, or one wonderfully specific little utility. This is my small way of returning the favor to developers, sysadmins, tinkerers, and fellow nerds.

“A shed is where useful things live, get sharpened, get rebuilt, and occasionally become something nobody planned.”

FadeShed is not a company and it is not a grand product suite. It is a home for practical software: small tools, odd utilities, experiments that survived contact with reality, and projects that became useful enough to clean up and share.

The common thread is simple: build the thing that removes friction, keep it understandable, and leave the door open for the next person who has the same problem.

If something here saves you a few minutes, an annoying workaround, or one late-night debugging session, the shed has done its job.

The shed rule

Practical beats impressive.

No roadmap theatre. No need to turn every useful script into a startup. If a tool solves a real problem, can be explained clearly, and might help someone else, it belongs here.

FadeShed — built by Fade, shared with the community.No trackers. No framework. Just HTML, CSS, and a shed full of tools.
